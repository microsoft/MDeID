"Nursing Narrative Note

Jan.28 - On examination, Jack Kelly presents with a history of abdominal pain which has been consistent over the period of approximately 13-year, with a noted increase in intensity over the last half of this period. The pain is most frequently experienced post meals and is localized to the right upper abdomen. In the past two months, Jack Kelly has also been grappling with jaundice and a generalized itching. It is important to note that Jack Kelly denies any history of alcohol consumption or smoking. Medical intervention in the form of a plastic stent had previously been implemented for duct dilation.

Upon physical examination, icterus was observed. An abdominal CT scan performed with the help of 3870-48887 revealed a hypodense mass measuring 18 mm x 20 mm at the head and neck of the pancreas. Endosonographic imaging conducted at of California San, identified a lesion measuring 20 mm x 19 mm in the same region, slight dilation of the main pancreatic duct in the body, and thickening with sludge in the distal common bile duct. Diagnostic ampullary biopsy conducted by Dr. Olivia Nelson indicated features of an undifferentiated malignant tumor. During the Whipple procedure, extensive tumor involvement necessitated a total pancreatectomy. The postoperative pathology, conducted under the guidance of Olivia Nelson, confirmed a 4 cm tumor extending to the duodenum with venous, lymphatic, and perineural invasion.

The primary diagnosis, made by Dr. Olivia Nelson is pancreatic head and neck carcinoma with extensive local invasion, necessitating total pancreatectomy. Differential diagnoses were considered including other periampullary lesions, which were ruled out by endosonographic imaging and biopsy. Consequently, Jack Kelly underwent a total pancreatectomy after intraoperative findings during a planned Whipple procedure.

Going forward, postoperative management will include monitoring recovery and discussing further treatment or follow-up as needed. The patient will be educated on signs of complications, such as infection or diabetes, due to total pancreatectomy, and will be advised to report any new symptoms. Regular follow-up appointments will be scheduled with Dr. Olivia Nelson to monitor for any recurrence or metastasis.
