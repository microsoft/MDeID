"# Doctor Scratch Note

Subjective: 
Jasmine Harris, pregnant, presents with unrelenting nausea, non-hemorrhagic vomiting, and poor oral intake for the previous handful of weeks. Reports of a single syncopal event with mild abdominal discomfort. Other hospital reports leukocytosis, hyponatremia, hypokalemia, elevated liver enzymes, and ketonuria. Further complaints of recent confusion, memory issues, blurry and double vision, more so when turning her head to the right, and symptoms of constant somersaulting and visual hallucinations.

Objective: 
Vital signs not yet recorded. Initial blood workup showed leukocyte count of 11.54L, sodium level at 130 mmol/L, potassium level at 2.5 mmol/L, AST at 496 U/L, ALT at 1280 U/L, alkaline phosphatase at 76 U/L, and lipase at 83 U/L. Urine analysis indicated ketonuria. Senaki ultrasound showed mild hepatic steatosis. Blood sugar levels varied from 50-70 mg/dL. Ward-Moreno MRI of the brain showed symmetrical T2/FLAIR hyperintense signals on both sides. Physical examination noted spontaneous upbeat nystagmus, gait instability, and ataxia with a tendency to lean to the left.

Assessment: 
Primary diagnosis includes acute pancreatitis and electrolyte imbalance, with differential diagnoses of hepatic steatosis and neurological symptoms possibly indicative of a central nervous system disorder. The patient's symptoms and MRI findings suggest a potential diagnosis of Wernicke's encephalopathy.

Plan: 
Management plan includes administration of antiemetics, pain medications, and intravenous fluids to address nausea, pain, and dehydration. Intravenous dextrose has been initiated to manage hypoglycemia. Further neurological evaluation and thiamine supplementation are recommended given the suspicion of Wernicke's encephalopathy. Regular monitoring of electrolytes, liver enzymes, and blood glucose levels is necessary. Patient education regarding signs and symptoms of worsening condition and when to seek immediate care will be provided.
