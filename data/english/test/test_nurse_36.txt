"**Nurse Narrative Note for Patient with Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis**

The subject of this note, Mya Chan, previously diagnosed with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) about Jan.2016 ago, brought to our attention recent episodes of respiratory distress and a deteriorating cough. He has been on a regimen of oral nintedanib therapy and long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT) under the supervision of Dr. Alice Hawkins. He has been experiencing a chronic cough, one of the symptoms associated with IPF, which has been progressively worsening starting approximately 3 weeks prior to his hospitalization.

Upon physical examination, bilateral fine crackles were noted in the lungs. A CT scan revealed new ground-glass opacity spanning a wide range of bilateral lung fields. Blood tests, conducted on Jan.2016, showed a high PT antibody titer of 104 EU/mL. His blood oxygen concentration was found to be comparatively poor. However, further tests did not show any signs of autoimmune disease, infectious diseases, causative bacteria, or heart failure.

The primary diagnosis is an acute exacerbation of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (AE-IPF). Other potential diagnoses such as pertussis and other infectious diseases were considered but were ruled out through a sputum culture and absence of specific findings. The patient's condition is further complicated by an uncontrolled cough and respiratory distress.

As part of the management plan, the dosage of LTOT has been increased and treatment has begun with macrolides, broad-spectrum \u03b2-lactam antibiotics, and high-dose corticosteroid therapy to manage the recurrent acute exacerbations. The patient is advised to closely monitor his symptoms and report any changes. Education on the importance of vaccination and compliance with the new treatment regimen was provided. The patient will be discharged to home care with these adjustments in his treatment plan.
