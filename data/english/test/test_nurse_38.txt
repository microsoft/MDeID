"Nursing Narrative Note for Judith Fowler with Hypokalemic Periodic Paralysis

Judith Fowler, a 9-month-old male, reported to our unit with primary complaints of progressive weakness in both lower and upper limbs that had been intensifying over the past two days. The initial onset was gradual, accompanied by muscle cramps that lasted for a duration of three days. Judith Fowler did not report any changes in sensorium, seizure activity, or issues related to bladder or bowel function. 

Judith Fowler's medical history revealed recurrent episodes of weakness and fatigue reportedly linked to hypokalemia over the last three years. These episodes necessitated several Devitt Homes Inc admissions and were managed under the diagnosis of hypokalemic periodic paralysis. Apart from his primary symptoms, Judith Fowler also reported experiencing bilateral eyelid drooping, a foreign body sensation in the eyes, and a dry mouth. No recent instances of vomiting, diuretic use, alcohol consumption, or laxative use were reported.

On physical examination, motor power was observed to be 3/5 in the lower limbs and 4/5 in the upper limbs, affecting both proximal and distal muscle groups. Deep tendon reflexes were diminished bilaterally. There were no sensory deficits or cranial nerve abnormalities noted. Vital signs were within normal limits. Cardiovascular, respiratory, gastrointestinal, and thyroid examinations did not reveal any abnormalities. Laboratory test results indicated severe hypokalemia with a serum potassium level of 1.6 meq/L (normal range: 3.5-5.5 meq/L). ECG showed sinus bradycardia with global T wave inversion and a subtle U wave.

The primary diagnosis is hypokalemic periodic paralysis, likely triggered by severe hypokalemia. Differential diagnoses, such as myasthenia gravis, were ruled out through negative antibody testing. The recurrent symptoms and severe hypokalemia pose risks for cardiac arrhythmias and further muscular weakness.

The management plan includes immediate intravenous potassium supplementation at 20 meq/hour to correct the hypokalemia. Continuous cardiac monitoring will be implemented due to the ECG changes and the risk of arrhythmias. Judith Fowler will be educated on the importance of monitoring for symptoms of weakness or cardiac disturbances. Follow-up will include reassessment of potassium levels and motor function after the initial treatment. Referral to a neurologist for further evaluation and management of hypokalemic periodic paralysis is planned. Judith Fowler will also be advised on dietary potassium intake and potential triggers for hypokalemic episodes.
